Node 01: Self-custody vs. collaborative custody vs. exchange

Premise

Bitcoin's properties, savings that can't be inflated and payments that can't be censored, only hold if you are the bearer. A custodian reintroduces the counterparty risk Bitcoin exists to remove, links your identity to your holdings, and makes you a named target for a wrench attack you didn't need to invite. Everything below assumes this as the starting point, not something up for debate.

The question

Given the premise above, what accountability are you actually taking on with full self-custody, versus collaborative custody (a co-signing service or third party holds one key in a multisig with you), versus leaving funds on an exchange? What did each option let you avoid, and what did it cost you?

One live position, offered to be tested here rather than assumed: collaborative custody is “abdication-as-a-service”. You are still trusting someone else's solvency, honesty, and continued willingness to cooperate, just with better branding than an exchange. Is that fair, or does collaborative custody solve a real problem (loss of a single key, no heir with technical skill) that pure self-custody doesn't?
