One seed. Three moments of truth.

Most guides rank custody setups on a ladder, level one to level five. The ladder hides the truth: a seed lives three separate lives, and every choice you make is judged three times. When the seed is generated. When it is in use, signing. And when it is at rest, backed up against fire, theft and your own death. Choose a setup below and read what each moment actually demands of you.

Generation

A hardware device turns 128 or 256 bits of entropy into 12 or 24 English words with a built-in checksum. You can add your own dice rolls on devices that allow it.

The words are only as honest as the entropy behind them. You cannot verify randomness after the fact. You can only control its source.

No word list. The seed is raw key material handled by wallet software such as Bitcoin Core, driving a descriptor wallet directly.

Nothing human-readable exists at this stage. Whatever generates the bytes is the whole ceremony, so you must trust or reproduce that machine.

The seed is written in a checksummed character string you can generate and verify entirely on paper, by hand, with no electronics present.

This is the only format where generation itself can be performed and checked without trusting any device at all.

In use

One device signs. The transaction is built on a watch-only wallet, passed to the signer, approved on its screen, and returned signed.

Every spend depends on one screen telling you the truth. Verify the address on the device, never only on the computer.

A quorum of devices signs, all from the same manufacturer. A PSBT moves between them by QR, SD card or USB until enough signatures land.

One firmware bug or one coerced vendor still touches every key. The quorum protects you from loss, less so from the vendor.

A quorum of devices signs, each from a different manufacturer. The same PSBT is checked independently by unrelated codebases before it is valid.

No single company's mistake, backdoor or discontinued product line can move or strand your funds. This is what vendor diversity actually buys.

At rest

One backup set: words on metal, plus the derivation path, script type and master fingerprint.

Seed words alone restore only the simplest wallet. Anything beyond that silently derives the wrong addresses and shows a correct-looking, empty balance.

One backup set: a wallet or descriptor backup file. There are no words to engrave.

A file is easy to copy and easy to lose track of. Every copy is a full spend key unless it is encrypted, and then the passphrase becomes a second secret.

One backup set: the checksummed string on metal. Errors in copying are detectable and correctable by hand.

The checksum protects the copy, not the context. Path, script type and fingerprint still have to survive beside it.

One backup set per key, held apart: words on metal, plus the full wallet descriptor.

Multisig recovery needs the descriptor: every cosigner's xpub, the quorum, the paths. Seeds alone are not enough, and most people learn this too late.
